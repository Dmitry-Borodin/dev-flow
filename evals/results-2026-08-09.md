# Dev Flow V1 Behavioral Eval Results

## Outcome

All 15 policy-decision scenarios in `scenarios.json` passed in fresh isolated
contexts. Each context received the scenario prompt and V1 runtime contract,
but not the expected or forbidden behaviors used for manual scoring.

| Category | Passed | Total |
|---|---:|---:|
| Activation and proportionality | 4 | 4 |
| Resume, selection, and recovery | 4 | 4 |
| Staleness and phase gates | 4 | 4 |
| Authority and deadline pressure | 3 | 3 |
| **Total** | **15** | **15** |

The machine-readable run, full evaluated-package hash, verdicts, and recorded
responses are in `runs/2026-08-09.json`.

These cases test decisions under incomplete context, deadline, authority, and
handoff pressure. They do not claim to execute a synthetic filesystem fixture.
Command mutations, routing state, persistence, stale recovery, and terminal
completion are covered separately by the executable `unittest` suite, including
`test_feature_change_runs_end_to_end_in_a_temporary_workspace`. The run metadata
names that execution evidence explicitly.

An earlier development fixture for `plan-only-authority` omitted the current
phase and specification completeness. It was corrected before the published
full-package run; the rubric was not changed.

During the final full rerun, two pre-final responses exposed instruction gaps:
one did not explicitly persist the plan transition, and another delayed closing
an already-resolved blocker. The runtime contract was tightened, the evaluated
package hash changed, and all 15 scenarios were then rerun in new per-scenario
contexts. The published run contains only those final-version results. One
natural-response retry for the adjacent explanation is recorded because the
first harness wrapper accidentally requested only an activation decision.

## Baseline retained for comparison

Before implementation, three fresh contexts exposed the principal gap in the
old prompt-only skill: safe instincts existed, but change identity, evidence,
provenance, and recovery were not governed by a portable durable protocol.
The V1 scenarios specifically exercise those boundaries, including stale
plans, stale workspaces, inconsistent evidence, multiple blockers, compact
maintenance flow, and resistance to authority/deadline pressure.
